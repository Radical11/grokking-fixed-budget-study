# Abstract

Grokking is delayed improvement on unseen examples after strong performance on training examples. This project studies the number of optimizer updates until a small transformer first confirms generalization on modular addition modulo 31. Across 120 independently initialized main-study runs, we compare two training-data fractions and three weight-decay settings. Success requires three consecutive evaluations with at least 99% training accuracy and 95% held-out accuracy. Unfinished event times remain unknown; statistical comparisons use the observed capped time U=min(T,30000). The study records 83 confirmed events and 37 unfinished outcomes. At the selected baseline, the mean confirmed time is 5481.25 updates and the median is 4025, showing substantial variation across starts. Candidate timing distributions, exploratory hypothesis comparisons, regression and ANOVA describe this variation with assumption checks. An empirical completion curve predicts success when a fixed budget is divided between one, two or four independent runs. Fresh-seed validation gives 10/10 successes for every policy at 30000 total logical updates, with exact 95% intervals of 69.2%-100%; this budget does not distinguish the policies. Later accuracy dips in 62 of 83 generalized runs also distinguish first confirmation from sustained success. The proposed contribution is a reproducible empirical connection between grokking timing and restart allocation, conditional on this task, architecture and dataset split.
